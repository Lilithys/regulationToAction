EU OVC - ICAAP information

Our approach to assessing internal capital adequacy is grounded in a comprehensive,
forward-looking financial forecast. We establish a three-year base case that integrates key
business drivers, including strategic growth targets, operational and hiring needs, and the
evolution of our investment pipelines. The capital requirement for this baseline scenario is
determined in accordance with the most recent Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process
(SREP) decision, ensuring our foundational plan is aligned with regulatory expectations.

Building on this forecast, we conduct a rigorous stress-testing process to ensure our
resilience against material risks. Three distinct stress scenarios are
developed—idiosyncratic, market-wide, and combined—which are complemented by a
macroeconomic scenario provided biannually by the European Banking Authority (EBA). By
quantifying the potential capital depletion under each of these scenarios, we determine the
appropriate level of Pillar 2 guidance needed to maintain financial stability. A reverse stress
test is also performed to identify the magnitude of stresses that would cause a breach of
our binding capital requirements, and to assess the likelihood of such events materializing.
This multi-layered methodology ensures our internal capital is not only adequate for our
strategic ambitions but also robust enough to withstand significant and varied shocks.

On an annual basis, the results of this Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process
(ICAAP) are submitted to De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB), along with supporting
documentation, as part of the Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process (SREP).

The managing board steers bunq’s risk management approach by approving risk
management policies at the Group level.

The purposes of the Group's risk management policies is to identify and analyze the risks
faced by the Group, to set appropriate risk limits and controls, and to monitor risks and
adherence to limits. The Group has specified its risk limits and articulated its appetite for
risk taking in a Risk Appetite Statement, which is reviewed and updated frequently and
approved by the Managing and Supervisory Board.

The risk management policies and systems are reviewed regularly to reflect changes in
market conditions and the Group’s activities. The Group, through its training and
management standards and procedures, aims to develop a disciplined and constructive
control environment in which all employees understand their roles and obligations.

The Group's Risk Committee oversees how management monitors compliance with the
Group’s risk management policies and procedures and reviews the adequacy of the risk
management framework in relation to the risks faced by the Group. The committee
regularly monitors subsidiaries based outside of the Netherlands to ensure compliance with
risk policies and to ensure sufficient safeguards are in place in Group subsidiaries.

The Risk Committee complements the oversight role of the Supervisory Board with
insights obtained by the System Checker (Internal Audit). bunq's System Checker serves as
our robust third line of defense. The internal team evaluates the sufficiency and efficiency of
our governance, risk management, and control systems. The System Checker ensures

Hedging and mitigation
Liquidity risk is mitigated rather than hedged in a derivative sense. Mitigants include the
size and quality of the HQLA buffer, the granularity of the deposit base and DGS coverage,
and the operational readiness to monetize the buffer. The continuing effectiveness of these
mitigants is reviewed in the annual ILAAP and tested through stress scenarios; collateral
monetization capability is tested at least annually.

Stress testing
bunq runs idiosyncratic, market-driven and combined stress scenarios, embedding climate
and environmental factors as well as guidance from supervisory expectations on liquidity
stress testing.

The point of failure is defined as a breach of the ECB minimum reserve requirement (i.e.
inability to repay a customer withdrawal). The survival period exceeds 12 months under all
scenarios across the budget horizon.

Contingency Funding Plan (CFP)
The CFP team comprises the permanent members of ALCO. Escalation is triggered by RAS
threshold breaches on the LCR, NSFR, asset encumbrance ratio or survival period, or by
qualitative assessment of an emerging liquidity event. The Treasurer (1st line) and Head of
Financial Risk (2nd line) continuously monitor the position. Activation of the CFP triggers
immediate notification by the CRO to the Management Board, the Supervisory Board and
DNB.

The CFP contains the following menu of mitigating actions, which include halting new
uncommitted investments, increasing freely withdrawable savings rates and/or term
deposit rates to retain and attract deposits, monetizing HQLA via Eurosystem refinancing or
secondary sale and other tradable securities via bilateral repo or sale. If those measures
were insufficient, bunq could source senior funding from another bank, securitize an
additional portion of its loan portfolio, or request additional shareholder funding.

Qualitative information
Concentration of funding and liquidity sources
bunq's funding base is highly granular: the largest 53 depositors (those with balances above
EUR 1m) total EUR 222m, or 2.6% of total deposits, and ~91% of total deposits sit below the
DGS limit. The primary source of funding is freely withdrawable retail savings; transactional
accounts (~20% of deposits) are non-interest-bearing and provide bunq's most stable and
cheapest funding source. Wholesale concentration is limited and dominated by
crypto-related corporate balances, to which a 100% LCR outflow factor and very
conservative NSFR treatment are applied.

Composition of the liquidity buffer
As at 31 December 2025, the consolidated liquidity buffer amounted to EUR 4,704 million,
comprising:

    ●​   ECB cash net of the minimum reserve requirement: EUR 3,989m (84.8%)
    ●​   HQLA Level 1 securities (SSA bonds): EUR 248.7m
    ●​   HQLA Level 2 securities (ABS): EUR 138.8m
    ●​   Non-HQLA but ECB-eligible (Solitaire I): EUR 279.7m



Derivative exposures and potential collateral calls
Derivative exposures are limited to plain vanilla pay-fixed interest rate swaps used to hedge
mortgage IRRBB. LCR and stress-test outflows for derivatives use a worst-of
mark-to-market move observed over the preceding two years.

Currency mismatch
EUR represents over 98.8% of bunq's funding. Non-EUR deposits (USD and GBP being
dominant) are substantially matched by short-dated assets in the same currency via
short-term assets and bank deposits. Currency-specific LCRs are monitored where
regulatorily required.
